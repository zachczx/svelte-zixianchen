# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

The portfolio serves three overlapping audiences:

- Hiring managers and recruiters assessing product, technology, and leadership judgment.
- Potential collaborators and project partners looking for someone who can think and build.
- Product and technical peers who arrive through a project or article.

Most visitors will scan first, then follow whichever form of evidence matters to them: work history, shipped projects, writing, or contact.

## Product Purpose

Show how Zixian thinks at work, builds outside it, and explains what he learns along the way.

Success means a visitor quickly understands that Zixian combines public-sector technology and service-delivery experience with hands-on software building and clear, candid writing.

## Positioning

I turn messy real-world problems into useful systems, then explain what I learned plainly.

This is the central idea that should connect Day, Night, and Musings.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary action: Read a project case study.
- Secondary action: Read the writing.
- Final action for an interested visitor: Send a note or connect on LinkedIn.
- The line to remember: "He notices real problems, builds useful things, and explains his judgment clearly."

Belief ladder:

1. Zixian has substantial experience across public-sector technology, policy, communications, and service delivery.
2. He can identify and frame real problems without inflating them.
3. He can design and ship useful systems himself.
4. He reflects honestly and communicates what he learned.
5. He would be valuable to hire, work with, or exchange ideas with.

Proof already available:

- Work history spanning policy, communications, technology, and service delivery.
- Live products and detailed project case studies.
- Public GitHub repositories.
- Technical, product, and reflective writing.
- Concrete examples of old projects evolving into current ones.

The site currently has no testimonials, client logos, or press proof, so it should earn trust through specificity and visible work.

## Brand Personality

Curious, practical, candid.

The voice is conversational and first-person, like explaining something to a thoughtful friend. It is confident when the evidence is clear, honest about uncertainty, and occasionally self-deprecating when the situation earns it.

The overall impression should be grounded competence with a restless interest in making things better.

## Anti-references

The portfolio should avoid:

- Generic developer portfolios built around skill badges and empty claims.
- Polished corporate personal-brand language.
- Terminal or cyberpunk styling used as shorthand for technical ability.
- Agency-style case studies with inflated outcomes or invented certainty.
- Long inventories of implementation details without a user or design decision.
- AI-polished prose that removes personal rhythm, uncertainty, and humour.

## Design Principles

1. Show the work before making the claim.
2. Begin with the real problem, then explain the judgment behind the response.
3. Treat Day, Night, and Musings as one connected body of work.
4. Let each project retain its own identity while the portfolio provides a consistent voice.
5. Write as if explaining the work to a thoughtful peer.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the baseline. Preserve keyboard navigation, visible focus states, readable contrast, reduced-motion behaviour, meaningful image descriptions, and complete usability across desktop and mobile viewports. Content should remain understandable without animation.
